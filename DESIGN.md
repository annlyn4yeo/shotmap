# Shotmap: design direction

Read this before changing anything visual. The tokens live in `src/styles/tokens.css`; this file explains why they are what they are.

## 1. Three directions, one choice

### A. Survey Sheet

A national-survey map from the mid twentieth century: vellum paper, prussian-blue ink, one vermilion for benchmarks, a hairline neatline round the sheet. Shots are map symbols in a legend, the pitch is a plot of ground with edge ticks, and the goal is a triangulation point.

### B. Terrace Riso

A 1980s fanzine run off on a risograph: newsprint stock, two flat inks (fluorescent orange and teal) that overprint into a third, halftone dots, slightly misregistered edges, fat grotesque headlines. Shots are stamped dots; busy areas darken where inks overlap. It is loud, warm and hand-made.

### C. Tactics Slate

The manager's board in the tunnel: dark slate, chalk lines with a soft edge, arrows and circles drawn by hand, a dusting of rubbed-out marks. Shots are chalk circles and crosses. It is intimate and familiar, and it leans hard on texture.

### The choice: A, Survey Sheet

Shot data is positions, magnitudes and categories, which is exactly what cartography has spent two centuries learning to encode. Map symbology was built to survive one-colour printing, so shape, size and hatching already carry meaning without colour; that gives us the colour-blind and greyscale requirement for free, as the system's native language rather than a patch. The geometry is honest: contours of constant shooting angle are circles through the two goalposts, so the "terrain" of a pitch is real and cheap to draw (five `<circle>` elements). On mobile it suits a thumb and a small screen because maps are read in the hand: dense hairlines, tiny mono labels, big marks. Everything is vector and flat colour, with no elevation, no blur and no raster texture, so it costs almost nothing. Riso needs overprint blending and grain to feel right; Slate needs texture. Both fight the performance budget. Survey Sheet gets its richness from line, type and restraint.

**Name.** Kept as Shotmap. It already says the concept, and the survey language (sheet, contour, legend) now gives it a vocabulary. Tagline: _Every shot, surveyed._ The O of the wordmark is the goal mark.

## 2. Colour

One light token set: vellum paper and prussian ink. There is no dark theme. `color-scheme: light` keeps native controls and scrollbars light even when the OS is dark. No pure black or white anywhere.

| Token                                               | Role                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--paper`, `--paper-2`, `--paper-3`                 | Page, inset or pitch surface, pressed or selected                |
| `--ink`, `--ink-2`, `--ink-3`                       | Body, secondary, captions and map labels                         |
| `--line`, `--line-2`, `--line-3`                    | Boundaries that must be seen, contours and rules, faint dividers |
| `--accent`, `--accent-ink`, `--accent-wash`         | Vermilion, text on vermilion, 12% tint                           |
| `--goal`, `--saved`, `--off`, `--blocked`, `--post` | Shot outcomes                                                    |

One accent, used everywhere: vermilion. It is also the goal colour, so the accent and "the best thing that happened" are the same signal. Outcome colours follow map convention (benchmark red, water blue, contour brown, woodland green, plus a slate for the neutral miss).

Rules the numbers enforce (`pnpm contrast` fails the build on any miss):

- Body ink and secondary ink: AAA (7:1) on all three papers.
- Captions and labels (`--ink-3`): AA (4.5:1) on all three papers.
- Anything that must be seen to understand the chart (`--line`, outcome marks, accent marks): at least 3:1 on every paper. Outcome colours are also 4.5:1 on `--paper`, so they can colour a label.
- `--line-2` and `--line-3` are decoration only (contours, hairlines).

## 3. Typography

Two families, both SIL OFL, self-hosted from Fontsource sources and subset by `pnpm fonts` (`scripts/fonts.mjs`).

| Family                                                      | Used for                                             | Why                                                                                                                                                        | Files                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Archivo** (variable, wght 400 to 800, width fixed at 100) | Everything readable: wordmark, headings, body, names | A grotesque with a drafted, utilitarian honesty; the heavy weights are strong on a small screen and the text weights are sturdy. Has real tabular figures. | `archivo-latin` 27 KB, `archivo-latin-ext` 14 KB |
| **Azeret Mono** (variable, wght 400 to 600)                 | Map labels, axis ticks, buttons, data captions       | Wide, geometric mono with character. Reads as plate lettering on a survey sheet.                                                                           | `azeret-mono-latin` 15 KB                        |

- Subset to Latin plus a few typographic marks, arrows and minus. A second `archivo-latin-ext` file (Latin Extended-A) covers player names (Szczęsny, Çalhanoğlu) and is only downloaded when such a character appears, via `unicode-range`.
- Only `archivo-latin` is preloaded. `font-display: swap` throughout.
- **Zero layout shift:** each family has a metric-matched fallback (`Archivo Fallback` on Arial at size-adjust 102.17%, `Azeret Mono Fallback` on Courier New at 108.32%, with ascent/descent overrides). `pnpm fonts` prints the numbers.
- No italics, so no italic files. Emphasis is weight.

**Scale** (fluid between 375 px and 1280 px, a `clamp()` per step, roughly a minor-third ratio on mobile widening to a perfect fourth on desktop):

| Step        | Mobile to desktop | Use                    |
| ----------- | ----------------- | ---------------------- |
| `--step--2` | 12 to 13 px       | Map labels, axis ticks |
| `--step--1` | 14 to 15 px       | Captions, notes        |
| `--step-0`  | 16 to 18 px       | Body                   |
| `--step-1`  | 20 to 24 px       | Lead, large numerals   |
| `--step-2`  | 25 to 32 px       | Section headings       |
| `--step-3`  | 31 to 43 px       | Match titles           |
| `--step-4`  | 39 to 57 px       | Feature numerals       |
| `--step-5`  | 49 to 80 px       | Wordmark               |

**Weights:** 400 body, 500 labels and UI, 650 subheads, 750 headings, 800 wordmark. **Tracking:** `-0.025em` on headings and display, `0` body, `0.14em` on uppercase mono map labels (`.label`). **Leading:** 0.92 display, 1.12 headings, 1.5 body.

**Numbers:** `.num` sets `tabular-nums lining-nums`. Use it for every minute, xG value, score and coordinate so columns align. Verified: Archivo's tabular figures are identical width (`1111` and `0000` both measure 91.7 px at 40 px).

## 4. Shot encoding

Size is xG. Shape is the outcome. Colour is a third, redundant channel. Nothing depends on colour: check marks in greyscale before changing them.

- **Size:** radius `3.6 * sqrt(xG)` pitch units, so a mark's area is proportional to xG, with a floor of 7 CSS px so a 0.01 shot stays visible and tappable. Taps use a 24 px nearest-mark hit test rather than the mark's own size.
- **Focus mode:** while a shot is hovered, focused or selected, every other mark drops to 28% opacity (opacity only, 160 ms).
- **One element per shot:** each mark is a single `<path>`. The goal uses four nested circles and the even-odd fill rule to get its ring, gap and centre hole.
- **Strokes** are in screen pixels (`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`), so marks stay crisp at any size.

| Outcome    | Shape                                                          | Fill                | Colour token         |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Goal**   | Solid disc, a separate outer ring, a paper-coloured centre dot | Solid               | `--goal` (vermilion) |
| Saved      | Ring with diagonal hatching                                    | Hatch plus 14% tint | `--saved`            |
| Off target | Cross                                                          | None                | `--off`              |
| Blocked    | Solid square                                                   | Solid               | `--blocked`          |
| Post       | Open triangle                                                  | 14% tint            | `--post`             |

The goal is the most special thing on screen on three counts. It is the only mark with two concentric elements and a hole. It is the only mark in the accent colour. It is drawn last, on top of everything, and plays a single expanding ring when it plots in. The same mark is the O in the wordmark.

Draw order is big marks first, small on top, goals last.

## 5. Pitch

Each team gets its own panel: the attacking end only, cropped to the last 40 yards (the crop is read from the data: 99% of shots in the open data are inside 35 yards, and both panels share it so they compare directly). Goal at the top, rotated so the attack runs up the screen. Where the pitch is cut there is no line: the surface fades in, so it reads as continuing. `Pitch` still supports `full` mode, both orientations and a `from` crop; the shot map uses half, vertical, cropped.

- **Surface:** flat `--paper-2` over `--paper`. No stripes, no grass, no raster.
- **Lines:** boundary and boxes in `--line` at 1.25 px; the goal frame in `--ink` at 2 px, the only heavy line, so the target reads first. Penalty spot a small dot. Corner arcs and the centre-circle arc are drawn; the halfway line closes the bottom.
- **Contours:** off by default (`contours` prop). Circles through both goalposts, in `--line-2`; they added noise to a busy shot map, so they are kept for a future lighter view.
- **Axis:** one scale only, yards from the goal line, down the left edge in mono.
- **Sheet frame:** a double rule (1.5 px plus 1 px, 3 px gap) built from `border` and `outline`. No shadow.

The whole pitch is a few dozen SVG elements, with no images.

## 6. Layout, shape, elevation

- **Space:** 4 px base, tokens `--space-1` to `--space-9` (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 px). Page gutter `clamp(1rem, 0.6rem + 1.7vw, 2rem)`, maximum width 72 rem, reading measure 62ch.
- **Mobile first:** 375 px is the design size. One column; the pitch fills the width. Multi-column layouts appear only where there is room, always as a deliberate recomposition.
- **Radius:** none. Surfaces, buttons and inputs are square. Circles are reserved for shot marks and nothing else, which makes a circle meaningful.
- **Borders:** hairlines (1 px) for structure, a heavy rule (1.5 px) in `--ink` to open a section, the double neatline for the main map.
- **Elevation:** deliberately absent. No shadows, no blur, no glass. Separation comes from rules and changes of paper tone. Overlays (tooltips, sheets) are inverted: ink background, paper text.
- **Layers:** only systemic (`--layer-sticky`, `--layer-overlay`).
- **Cards:** avoided. Group with rules and space.

## 7. Motion

Motion is the pen plotting the sheet, not decoration. Everything is CSS (`transform`, `opacity`); no animation library.

| Token           | Value                             | Use                                                              |
| --------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--dur-press`   | 120 ms                            | Press feedback: `scale(0.97)` on `:active`                       |
| `--dur-fast`    | 160 ms                            | Hover, colour changes                                            |
| `--dur-base`    | 240 ms                            | A mark plotting in (opacity and scale from 0.6)                  |
| `--dur-slow`    | 480 ms                            | Sheets, route changes                                            |
| `--dur-draw`    | 900 ms                            | Contours drawing in, once per view                               |
| `--ease-out`    | `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`  | Anything entering or responding                                  |
| `--ease-in-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1)` | Things moving across the screen                                  |
| `--stagger`     | 18 ms                             | Between marks, capped at 28 marks (about half a second in total) |

**What animates, and why.** Marks plot in with a short stagger so a match reads as events in sequence (storytelling). A goal adds one expanding ring, once (hierarchy). Contours fade in (orientation). Buttons depress under the finger (feedback). Filter changes crossfade in 150 ms or less, never replay the plot: the user will do it dozens of times.

**What does not animate.** Keyboard-driven actions. Anything repeated constantly. No scroll-jacking, no parallax, no looping effects.

**Reduced motion.** All movement is gated behind `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`. With reduce, marks and contours simply appear in place, and nothing else changes.

**Interruptibility.** State changes use CSS transitions so they retarget mid-flight. Keyframes are used only for one-shot entrances.

## 8. Foundation, as built

- `src/styles/tokens.css`: every variable above.
- `src/styles/fonts.css`: `@font-face` for both families, the lazy extended file and the two fallbacks.
- `src/styles/base.css`: reset, element defaults, `.num`, `.label`, focus ring (2 px accent, 3 px offset), selection.
- `src/components/`: `ShotMark`, `Pitch`, `Legend`, `Wordmark`, each with its own small CSS file.
- `scripts/contrast.mjs` (`pnpm contrast`) checks the pairs above; `scripts/fonts.mjs` (`pnpm fonts`) regenerates the subsets and fallback metrics.
